02 / WORK IN PROGRESS

Prototype write-up

What have you done so far?

I selected the historical Austin Animal Center Outcomes dataset and narrowed the analysis to cat outcome records. I cleaned age descriptions into kitten, adult, and senior categories and organized coat-color labels into comparable groups. I explored six static charts for the proposal, including adoption outcome counts, age comparisons, coat comparisons, and a heatmap. For this initial prototype, I used D3.js to create linked bar charts with filters for age, coat color, and calendar year. I also added visible sample sizes and a consistent definition of adoption outcome share so the charts are interpretable.

What will be the most challenging to design, and why?

The most challenging part will be helping readers compare adoption outcomes without overstating what the records can tell us. Age and coat color are not independent of other factors, and a simple difference in percentages does not establish a causal effect. I also need to choose interactions that reveal meaningful differences while preventing readers from confusing recorded outcomes with individual cats' adoption chances. Another challenge is making subgroup sizes visible because some categories are much smaller than others. For the final version, I plan to add a stronger guided narrative, clearer annotations, and comparisons that help readers notice how results shift after changing filters.

About these records

Source: City of Austin — Austin Animal Center Outcomes (historical archive, 10/01/2013–05/05/2025). This prototype uses only full calendar years 2014–2024. Data are grouped counts from the public CSV, not generated or simulated. Outcomes are shelter encounter records; individual Animal IDs may appear more than once. Cohort differences do not show cause and effect. Charts are drawn in D3.js and updated when filters change.
